# Sponsorship, advertising, and affiliate policy

Status at launch: **no sponsors, no ads, no affiliate links**.

This policy exists now so future monetization cannot silently alter scientific incentives.

## Acceptable future support

If the Commons gains traction, compatible support may include:

- donated API/inference credits;
- donated CPU/GPU/HPC capacity;
- grants to a transparent community compute pool;
- clearly labelled sponsor acknowledgements;
- clearly labelled static affiliate links to relevant coding/inference plans.

The preferred model is **compute support for public work**, not attention-maximizing advertising.

## Non-negotiable independence

Money, credits, sponsorship, advertising, or affiliate relationships must never affect:

- problem/frontier priority;
- evidence status;
- source ordering;
- reviewer selection or review conclusions;
- acceptance/rejection/dispute decisions;
- formalization semantics;
- claims about model quality;
- whether a sponsor's model/service is recommended for a particular problem.

No sponsor may purchase "official solver," "preferred model," theorem status, reviewer status, or scientific endorsement.

## Presentation

Any future commercial link must:

- be clearly labelled `Sponsor`, `Affiliate`, or `Compute supporter`;
- disclose compensation/affiliate status adjacent to the link;
- use `rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer"` where applicable;
- avoid third-party tracking/advertising JavaScript by default;
- not interrupt mathematical or review surfaces;
- not appear inside evidence/claim cards in a way that could imply endorsement.

A dedicated future `/support/` or `/compute/` surface is preferable to ads in scientific content.

## Compute pool

If sponsor traction exists, the best-aligned use is a public compute pool:

`sponsor/donor credits → declared pool → bounded public problems → provenance-preserving attempts → review`

Allocation rules and consumption should be auditable, and sponsorship must not buy priority.

## Affiliate rankings

The Commons must not publish rankings such as "best coding model" if the ordering is influenced by affiliate economics.

If comparative model/tool evaluations are ever published, their protocol, budgets, tasks, failures, and conflicts must be disclosed independently of commercial relationships.
