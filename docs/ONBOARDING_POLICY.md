# Audience and onboarding policy

## Goal

Navier–Stokes Commons should be usable by people with radically different starting points without flattening the mathematical frontier.

Understanding depth and participation capability are separate axes. A visitor can know little PDE while still be an excellent programmer, formalizer, numerical researcher, reviewer, educator, or owner of useful compute.

The website therefore exposes an explicit **Start here** path before requiring visitors to understand the internal vocabulary of research programs, problems, attempts, artifacts, evidence, and review.

## Entry routes

The Start page must have obvious paths for:

- curiosity/time only;
- first-time coding-agent user;
- existing AI/inference subscription or API credits;
- spare CPU/GPU/local compute;
- programmer/software engineer;
- Lean/formal-method user;
- CFD/numerical researcher;
- graduate mathematician/PDE researcher;
- skeptical reviewer/falsifier.

No route should claim that all contributions are mathematically equivalent.

## First-time agent principle

Installing a coding agent is **not** a prerequisite to participate.

A newcomer can:

1. read the public explainer;
2. take an L0 source/wording check;
3. use an ordinary browser assistant;
4. later move to a repo-capable coding/research agent.

Any coding agent should consume the same canonical machine interface:

`.well-known/commons.json` → `SKILL.md` → bounded problem/work packet → artifact → review.

The Commons is vendor-neutral. Product links may be provided as convenience, not endorsement.

## Scientific-status presentation

As of 11 September 2026 CMI publicly said it was contemplating an announcement that the problem had **"apparently been settled"**, while explicitly saying its evaluation and credit process is deliberately unhurried. That is a stronger institutional signal than silence, but is not prize recognition and does not close unforced A/B.

## Casual-first success criterion

A visitor with no mathematics degree and no coding-agent experience should be able to answer within five minutes:

1. What happened?
2. What remains open?
3. Why is the Commons useful?
4. What can I personally contribute?
5. What can my AI subscription or idle compute contribute?
6. What is the smallest safe first step?
7. Why does an AI-generated result not automatically become scientific truth?
