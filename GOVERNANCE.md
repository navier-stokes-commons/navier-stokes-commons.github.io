# Governance

## 1. Scope

This policy governs the bootstrap open beta. It is deliberately small enough to operate immediately and strict enough to prevent a maintainer, contributor, or agent from converting participation into scientific authority.

## 2. Roles

### Bootstrap maintainer

The repository owner acts as bootstrap maintainer until maintainers are explicitly recorded. The maintainer may publish or retire seed quests, triage proposals, request review, merge maintenance changes after required checks, and merge accepted records after their review gates pass.

The maintainer may not unilaterally label the maintainer's own contested scientific result as independently reviewed.

### Mission captain

A mission captain curates scope, decomposes a mission into quests, monitors dependencies, and recommends reviewers. Captaincy is stewardship, not ownership. Parallel attempts remain allowed.

### Contributor

A contributor may open attempts, produce results, propose missions or quests, and review work within competence. Human, agent, and human-agent contributions are admissible when provenance is disclosed.

### Reviewer

A reviewer records scope, competence, conflicts, evidence checked, verdict, and residual uncertainty. A review has authority only inside that declared scope.

## 3. Decisions

Public decisions use one of: `accept`, `request-changes`, `reject`, `supersede`, `reopen`, `defer`.

Scientific acceptance requires the quest-specific checks and at least one independent competent reviewer. High-consequence or disputed claims may require additional reviewers. Maintenance artifacts may use lighter review when the quest says so.

## 4. Appeals and disagreement

Any contributor may open a review or falsification targeting a decision. A conflicted decision-maker may not be the sole adjudicator of the appeal. Dissent that remains technically relevant should be linked from the public record rather than erased.

## 5. Mission admission

Maintainer seed missions are marked `active-seed`. New community missions enter `public-rfc`. Promotion to `active` requires a precise question, non-duplicative scope, public starting evidence, falsifiable or checkable acceptance conditions, and a viable review class.

## 6. Status authority

Repository labels, issue state, or contributor reputation do not by themselves establish a scientific claim. Claim status is controlled by the evidence and review record represented in canonical public data.

## 7. Change control

Changes to schemas, acceptance semantics, claim status rules, licenses, or public/private boundaries require a recorded decision and successful release gates. Breaking schema changes require a version increment and migration note.
